Lehman Brothers reported a $3.9B profit in 2007. Six months into 2008 it reported a $2.4B loss.

This report reads Lehman's 2007 annual report and Q2 2008 filing through financial ratios. The question: were the warning signs already in the numbers?

Why it failed

Excessive leverage

Leverage passed 30x in 2007. Debt to equity reached 672%. Equity was a thin layer over a very large balance sheet.

Overexposure to risky assets

MBS and ABS holdings rose from $57.7B to $89.1B in 2007. Real estate held for sale rose from $9.4B to $21.9B. Derivatives doubled from $22.7B to $47.0B by Q2 2008. Safe government and agency securities fell from $47.3B to $27.0B.

Weak liquidity

The current ratio never rose above 0.46. The quick ratio stayed near 0.2. The firm relied on short-term repo funding, so it could not roll over debt once lenders pulled back.

Low quality earnings

ROE of 17.5% in 2007 came from leverage and marked-to-market structured assets. Lehman delayed write-downs that rivals took in late 2007. This hurt trust. Interest coverage fell from 1.2 to 0.78 by Q2 2008.

No government backstop

Barclays and Bank of America withdrew when no federal support was offered, unlike the Bear Stearns rescue. Bankruptcy followed on 15 September 2008.

Lessons going forward

Leverage and liquidity ratios gave earlier warnings than profit did. Analysts should read profit together with funding structure, asset quality and interest coverage. After 2008, rules such as Dodd-Frank (2010) and tighter capital and liquidity requirements pushed banks in this direction.
